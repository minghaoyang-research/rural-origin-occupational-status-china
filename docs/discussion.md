# Teacher training, recruitment and later qualifications

A teacher who entered with junior college and later earned a bachelor's was already a graduate when hired, whereas someone who started below junior-college level and completed junior college during employment entered the graduate population only later. CGSS records the year of the highest award. I want to find out how common the second route was among rural-origin teachers.

## Routes available at different times

Article 9 of the [1999 continuing-education regulation](https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A02/s5911/moe_621/199909/t19990913_180474.html) includes qualification-raising education for employed primary and secondary teachers. The [2002 guidance](https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A10/s7058/200209/t20020910_162697.html) also describes preparation before appointment, including a transitional 3+2 route beginning with 中师 (secondary normal education).

The [Special Post scheme and free teacher education programme](references.md), introduced in 2006 and 2007 respectively, could have shaped later routes into teaching through recruitment and training, but CGSS does not identify participants.

## A county study

I need the earlier certificates. A county file that keeps only the latest qualification leaves the same gap as CGSS. For the proposed study, I would link the first higher-education certificate to the first appointment record for primary and secondary teachers from several recruitment periods, including former teachers where reachable and teachers still studying for a higher award. Interviews can fill gaps left by transfers or interrupted study. The linkage needs approved access and consent; publications would omit names and school identifiers.

A study of recruitment also needs applications, offers and acceptances for people who took teaching jobs and for applicants who took other work or found no work, together with their earlier attainment and family circumstances. Rural representation among applicants to and entrants into teacher-training programmes can be compared with representation at each of these recruitment stages.

An appointment does not tell me which job someone wanted. In Wang and Gao's ([2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tate.2013.01.008)) interviews with 19 students at one pilot university, the students' ambitions for social mobility could conflict with the Free Teacher Education programme's aim of staffing rural schools.

## The income comparison within teaching

Recording salary and hours for each job and year, including earlier posts, would allow comparisons at similar career stages and avoid pairing income from one job with the occupation recorded at a later date. Wu and Xie ([2003](https://doi.org/10.1177/000312240306800306)) distinguished established workers from later sector entrants using records from 1987 and 1996.

Interviews about establishment status (*bianzhi*), contract terms and competing offers at appointment can help distinguish teachers who preferred a secure post from those who had few other options. The interviews would also ask what teachers value about a particular school or post beyond its salary, drawing on Li's [Chinese prestige study (2005)](https://shxyj.ajcass.com/Admin/UploadFile/20130926008/2015-08-24/Issue/asqbvcrk.pdf), which connects occupational standing with authority and work-unit characteristics as well as education and income.
